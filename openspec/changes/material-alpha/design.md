## Context

`<material color>` builds a `StandardMaterial` in `bevy-ts-tsx/src/intrinsics.rs`, cached by props in `IntrinsicAssets`, so equal props share one handle. `StandardMaterial::default()` is `Opaque`, which ignores alpha. `<material>` props cannot be bindings (`resolve.rs` refuses them); component fields can, and a binding writes the field each frame.

A glTF scene root is an entity with `WorldAssetRoot`. Its meshes are the instance's own entities, each with a `MeshMaterial3d<StandardMaterial>` whose handle is the glTF's labelled material, shared by every instance of that model. `scene-material` (lands first, same crate) walks them: `scene_entities(world, root)` returns the instance's entities, empty until ready, and it re-runs on `WorldInstanceReady`. It swaps matching meshes to a `ScriptMaterial3d` and keeps their originals in `SceneMaterialTargets` on the root.

## Decisions

1. **Names follow Bevy: `alpha`, `alphaMode`, `SceneAlpha`.** Bevy calls it alpha everywhere (`Color::alpha`, `with_alpha`, `AlphaMode`); "opacity" appears on no Bevy material. `alphaMode` reuses `kinds::AlphaDecl` and its parser and the `AlphaMode` TS type from `bevy-ts/material`, so one parser owns the names.
2. **The default mode is Bevy's own rule.** `StandardMaterial::from(Color)` picks `Blend` below alpha 1 and `Opaque` otherwise. `<material>` does the same on its final alpha, so `alpha={0.5}` alone is see-through. `StandardMaterial::default()` stays `Opaque` in Bevy because it has no colour yet; we always have one. An author who wants a cutout says `alphaMode={{ mask: 0.5 }}`. Side effect: an existing `color="#rrggbbaa"` with alpha below 1 now blends, which is what it asked for.
3. **The fade is a component, not a `<material>` prop.** A root has no material of its own; `SceneAlpha` says what it does to the scene. Being a layout-tier component (`#[derive(Component, Reflect, Default)]`, one `f32` field), it is bindable with no new binding path, writable from TS systems, and usable from Rust. `<material>` props stay unbindable: a bound `<material alpha>` would make one cached asset per distinct value. `alpha` is clamped, not refused, because a binding writes it every frame.
4. **One copy per distinct original per root, edited in place.** The originals are shared by every instance of the model, so fading them would fade every house. On first sync a root clones each distinct original its meshes hold and points those meshes at the copy. `SceneAlphaTargets` on the root keeps `mesh -> original` and `original -> copy`. A changed `SceneAlpha` (`Changed<SceneAlpha>`) rewrites only the copies' `base_color` alpha and `alpha_mode`, skipping a copy whose values are already right. No asset is added or dropped while the fade runs. Removal points each mesh still holding a copy back at its original and drops `SceneAlphaTargets`, which frees the copies.
5. **Per-frame cost.** A copy edited is a `StandardMaterial` re-prepared by the renderer: its uniform data rewritten, no pipeline work. The mode changes only when alpha crosses 1, which re-specializes once. For the agent-town case, 10 roots times their distinct materials (Synty buildings share a few atlas materials, so tens, not hundreds) re-prepare per frame for 2 s. The cost that lasts is batching: while a root holds copies its meshes no longer share a material with other instances of the model, so they draw in their own batches. It ends when `SceneAlpha` is removed or the root despawns. A root at alpha 1 still holds copies; docs say to remove the component when not fading.
6. **The seam with scene-material** (agreed with build-scene-material, 2026-10-23). `alpha.rs` calls `scene_entities`. scene-material does all its swaps and restores in `SceneMaterialSet`, a public `SystemSet` in `PostUpdate`; its `WorldInstanceReady` observer only records the root. `alpha.rs` does the same: its observer records the root, and its sync is a `PostUpdate` system `.after(SceneMaterialSet)`, so scene-material's commands are applied before it reads. A mesh with a `ScriptMaterial3d` has no `StandardMaterial` and is skipped. The one overlap is ours to own: scene-material may swap a mesh that holds our copy and record the copy as its original in `SceneMaterialTargets` (public). On `SceneAlpha` removal, `alpha.rs` rewrites any copy in that root's `SceneMaterialTargets` back to its original. When scene-material restores while `SceneAlpha` is on, the mesh gets our copy back, which is right.
7. **Hot reload.** A glTF saved again: Bevy replaces its labelled materials in place and respawns or re-readies the instance. On `AssetEvent::Modified` of an original, its copy is rebuilt from the new value; on `WorldInstanceReady`, stale targets are dropped and the walk runs again. A TS save keeps the entity and re-applies `SceneAlpha` as a component field, so `Changed` runs the sync. `<material alpha>` is a prop like `color`: a new value is a new cached material.
8. **Protocol.** Untouched: no editor method or event changes. `SceneAlpha` shows in the inspector as any reflected component.

## Risks

- Blended buildings sort per mesh, so overlapping parts of one building may draw in the wrong order mid-fade. Accepted for a 2 s fade; premultiplied or dithered fades are follow-ups.
- Bevy casts no shadow from `Blend` meshes, so a building's shadow vanishes as the fade starts.
- A system that gives a scene mesh its own material while faded is overwritten on removal. Accepted, as in scene-material: the scene's meshes belong to the scene.
